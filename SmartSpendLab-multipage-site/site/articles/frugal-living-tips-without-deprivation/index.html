10 Frugal Living Hacks That Will Save You Hundreds Every Month

Spend on what matters, cut what doesn’t.

By SmartSpendLab Editorial TeamUpdated October 22, 20263 min read

Frugal living is often confused with going without. A better description is spending deliberately: paying full price for the things that matter to you and cutting what does not. Done well, it feels like control rather than sacrifice. The ten habits below are practical, easy to start and add up to meaningful monthly savings.

Ten habits that save money without misery

  1. Plan meals weekly. Food waste is expensive. A simple plan and shopping list cut impulse purchases and unused ingredients. Our grocery guide has more detail.
  2. Choose store brands. For staples such as flour, pasta, rice and tinned goods, the quality gap is often small and the price gap large.
  3. Wait 48 hours on non-essential purchases. If you still want it after two days, buy it with a clear conscience.
  4. Borrow before you buy. Libraries lend books, audiobooks, films and often tools, games and museum passes.
  5. Review insurance and phone plans yearly. Providers often reward new customers more than loyal ones. Compare offers and call your current provider with the best quote in hand.
  6. Reduce energy use. LED bulbs, smart plugs for standby devices and sealing draughts lower bills with very little effort.
  7. Buy secondhand. Furniture, clothing, tools and sports gear can often be found in good condition for much less.
  8. Cancel what you do not use. Start with the subscription audit.
  9. Cook in batches. Making a large pot once and freezing portions saves money and time on busy days.
  10. Use cashback tools carefully. They help if you were going to buy anyway. They hurt if they make you buy more.

How to start without burning out

Do not try all ten at once. Choose three that fit your life and track the savings for a month. For example, planning meals might save $30 a week, cancelling two subscriptions might save $25 a month, and a yearly insurance review might save $200 or more. Small wins build the habit and make the next step easier.

Know your value-based spending

A helpful exercise is to list the five things you would be most unhappy to give up: perhaps coffee with friends, a hobby, travel or a particular streaming service. Protect those in your budget on purpose. Then look hard at everything else. This turns frugality from a rule you resent into a choice you made.

Avoid false economies

  • Buying the cheapest version of something you use daily and replacing it twice.
  • Driving across town for a small discount that costs more in fuel and time.
  • Stockpiling bulk offers you will not finish before they expire.
  • Skipping essential maintenance, such as servicing a car, which can lead to far larger bills.

Where the savings should go

Savings only help if they are directed. Automate a transfer for the amount you save so it does not leak back into spending. Early on, use it for a starter emergency fund; later, for debt or investing. Saving $30 a week adds up to over $1,500 a year, and with the compound interest calculator you can see how regular amounts grow over time.

Frugality works best when it supports a goal you care about. Know what you are saving for, and the small choices get much easier.
