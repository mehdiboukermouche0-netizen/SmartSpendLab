Debt Snowball vs. Debt Avalanche: Which Strategy Fits You Best?

Compare the two best payoff strategies.

By SmartSpendLab Editorial TeamUpdated October 22, 20263 min read

If you have more than one debt, the order in which you pay them off matters. The two best-known strategies are the debt avalanche and the debt snowball. Both work. The right choice depends on whether you are motivated more by maths or by momentum.

How the avalanche method works

List all your debts by interest rate. Pay the minimum on every debt, then put every spare dollar toward the debt with the highest rate. When it is cleared, move its payment to the debt with the next highest rate. Because you attack the most expensive debt first, you pay the least total interest.

How the snowball method works

List your debts by balance, smallest first. Pay the minimum on all of them, then put every spare dollar toward the smallest balance. When it is paid off, roll its payment into the next smallest. You may pay somewhat more interest, but you eliminate whole debts sooner, and each win builds motivation.

A worked example

Imagine three debts: a credit card with $3,000 at 24% APR, a car loan with $6,000 at 9% and a personal loan with $1,500 at 15%. Minimum payments are $90, $150 and $45, and you can add $200 a month on top.

ApproachTime to debt-freeTotal interest
Minimums onlyAbout 50 monthsAbout $3,550
Snowball (smallest balance first)About 25 monthsAbout $1,510
Avalanche (highest rate first)About 25 monthsAbout $1,390

In this example both methods finish at about the same time and the avalanche saves roughly $120 in interest. The bigger lesson is that paying extra at all saves around $2,000 compared with minimums only. The exact gap between the methods depends on how different the interest rates and balances are in your own situation.

Which method fits you?

  • Choose the avalanche if you are driven by numbers, you have a large gap between your highest and lowest interest rates, or a high-rate debt is large.
  • Choose the snowball if you have several small debts, you have tried and failed to stick with a plan before, or you need visible progress to stay motivated.
  • Blend them. Some people clear one or two tiny debts first for quick wins, then switch to the avalanche order.

Steps to start this week

  1. List every debt with its balance, interest rate and minimum payment.
  2. Work out how much extra you can pay each month. Our bill negotiation guide and subscription audit can help you find it.
  3. Choose your method and set up automatic minimum payments on every debt, so you never miss one.
  4. Direct the extra payment to your target debt on payday.
  5. When a debt is cleared, roll its full payment into the next one.

Do not forget these points

  • Keep a small starter emergency fund so a surprise does not push you back into debt.
  • Ask whether you qualify for a lower rate or a balance transfer, but check the fees and the rate after any promotional period.
  • Avoid adding new debt while paying down the old.
