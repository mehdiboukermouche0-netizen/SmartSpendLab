7 Unnecessary Monthly Subscriptions You Should Cancel Today

Find the quiet charges draining your budget.

By SmartSpendLab Editorial TeamUpdated October 22, 20263 min read

Subscriptions are designed to be forgotten. A few dollars here and there never feels painful, yet a typical household can easily pay for a dozen recurring services. At $10 each that is $120 a month, or $1,440 a year, for things you may barely use. The good news is that an honest audit takes about half an hour and can pay for itself the same day.

Seven subscriptions worth checking first

  1. Streaming services. Most people use one or two regularly. Keep the one you watch most and rotate others in for a month at a time when a show you want arrives.
  2. Gym memberships. If you have not visited in the last two months, the real cost per visit is probably very high. Cancel, or switch to a pay-as-you-go option, and use free alternatives such as walking, running or bodyweight workouts.
  3. Extra cloud storage. Check what you are storing. Deleting duplicate photos and old backups can bring you back under the free tier.
  4. Duplicate apps. Two music services, two note-taking apps or two VPNs is one too many. Pick one and cancel the other.
  5. News and magazine plans. Many local libraries give free digital access to major newspapers and magazines.
  6. Meal kits. If boxes pile up unused or you skip deliveries, the per-meal cost is not worth it.
  7. Free trials that converted. Trials that ask for a card are built to roll into a paid plan. Check your statement for charges you do not recognise.

How to run a 30-minute audit

Download the last two or three months of statements from your bank and card accounts. Highlight every charge that repeats, then list each one with its monthly cost. Next to each, write the last date you used it. If you cannot remember, that is a strong sign it can go. Cancel what you do not use, then decide whether the remaining ones are worth their price.

Annual charges are easy to miss, so look back twelve months where you can. A yearly software renewal or domain fee that appears once will not show in a short review.

Cancelling without the runaround

  • Look for the cancel option in account settings first. Many services let you cancel online.
  • If a company makes cancelling difficult, take screenshots of each step and keep any confirmation email.
  • Check whether you are in a contract period or whether cancelling takes effect at the end of the billing cycle.
  • If a charge continues after cancelling, contact your bank or card issuer. In many countries you can dispute charges you did not authorise.

Prevent subscription creep

Use a separate card or a calendar reminder for free trials, so you cancel before the first charge. Add a quarterly reminder to repeat the audit. Before signing up for anything new, ask whether it replaces something you already pay for.

What to do with the savings

Cancelling $60 a month frees $720 a year, but only if the money goes somewhere useful. Move the amount to savings on the same day as the cancellation takes effect. If you are starting from zero, that money can build your first $1,000 in under two years, and much sooner alongside other changes.
