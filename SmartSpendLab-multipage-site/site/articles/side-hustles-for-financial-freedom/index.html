5 Smart Side Hustles to Boost Your Savings This Year

Realistic ways to add income around a job.

By SmartSpendLab Editorial TeamUpdated October 22, 20264 min read

A side hustle works best when it fits your schedule, uses skills you already have and has low startup costs. Earnings below are rough ranges and vary widely.

HustleStartupTypical pace
Freelance writing or designLowProject-based
Virtual assistantLowHourly

1. Freelance your skill

Offer writing, design, editing or development. Start with three sample projects and one clear niche.

2. Virtual assistant

Businesses pay for inbox, scheduling and admin help. Reliability is your selling point.

3. Online tutoring

If you’re strong in a subject, students and parents pay for focused help.

4. Digital products

Budget spreadsheets, planners and templates take effort once and can sell repeatedly.

5. Reselling

Find underpriced items locally and list them online. Track costs and fees carefully.

Make it count

  • Send side income straight to savings or debt.
  • Track it separately and set money aside for taxes.
  • Start with one hustle and give it 90 days.

How to choose the right hustle

The best side hustle is the one you will actually keep doing. Start by listing the skills you already have, the hours you can realistically spare each week and how much money you can invest upfront. Then compare options on three factors: startup cost, time to first payment and how well the work fits around your main job. Freelance skills usually start paying faster, while digital products and reselling take longer to build but can scale.

Getting your first customer

  • Build proof. Create two or three sample projects or a simple profile that shows what you can do.
  • Pick a clear niche. “Social media graphics for local cafés” is easier to sell than “design”.
  • Start with your network. Tell friends, former colleagues and community groups what you offer.
  • Use marketplaces. Freelance platforms, tutoring sites and local classifieds make it easier to find buyers.
  • Price fairly, then raise rates. Charging too little attracts the wrong customers and burns you out.

Realistic expectations

Your first month will likely be slow. Most successful hustlers earn modestly at first, collect reviews and referrals, and then raise prices. Treat the first three months as an experiment. Track hours and income so you can see your real hourly rate and decide whether the hustle is worth continuing or scaling.

Taxes and rules you should not ignore

Side income is usually taxable. Keep records of earnings and expenses, set aside a share of each payment for tax (many people reserve 20% to 30%, but the right figure depends on your country and income) and check whether you must register as self-employed. Also read your employment contract in case it restricts outside work. Rules vary between the US, UK, Canada and Australia, so confirm the details with official government guidance or a qualified professional.

Managing your time

Most people can manage five to ten hours a week. Block the time in your calendar, use evenings or weekend mornings, and protect your sleep and your main job. A hustle that wrecks your health is not worth the extra income.

A simple 30-day launch plan

  • Week 1: choose one hustle and define your offer and price.
  • Week 2: create samples or a basic profile and set up a separate account for the money.
  • Week 3: contact 20 potential customers or list your first items.
  • Week 4: deliver, ask for feedback and a review, then refine your process.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not underprice, do not try five hustles at once, do not mix side income with everyday spending and do not skip tax planning. Avoid any opportunity that asks for upfront fees or promises guaranteed income, because those are common warning signs of scams.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best side hustle for beginners?

Pick the one that matches a skill you already have. Tutoring, virtual assistance and freelance writing have low startup costs.

How should I use the extra money?

Send it to a goal immediately: an emergency fund, high-interest debt or investing. Our debt payoff calculator shows what extra income can do.

Can I do this alongside a full-time job?

Usually yes, but check your contract and keep hours manageable.

Taxes, records and getting paid

Side income is usually taxable, and tracking it from day one saves stress later. Keep a simple record of every payment received and every business expense, and set money aside from each payment so a tax bill never surprises you. Many freelancers reserve roughly a quarter to a third of their earnings, but the right amount depends on your country, your other income and the deductions you qualify for. Check your local tax authority's guidance or speak to a qualified accountant.

Mistakes that slow people down

  • Starting three hustles at once. Each one needs a ramp-up period. Focus on a single option long enough to judge it fairly.
  • Underpricing out of fear. Work out the hourly rate you need after costs and fees, and use it as your floor.
  • Spending the income as it arrives. Decide in advance where side earnings go, for example 70% to savings or debt and 30% to enjoy.
  • Ignoring your main job's rules. Check your employment contract for conflict-of-interest or outside-work clauses.
