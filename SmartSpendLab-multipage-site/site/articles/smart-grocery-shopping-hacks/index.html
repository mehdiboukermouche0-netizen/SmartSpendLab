How to Slash Your Monthly Grocery Bill by 30% Without Starving

Eat well for less.

By SmartSpendLab Editorial TeamUpdated October 22, 20263 min read

For most households, groceries are one of the biggest flexible costs. Rent and loan payments are fixed, but food spending can move by hundreds of dollars a month depending on how you shop. A 30% reduction is realistic for many people who change several habits at once, without eating worse. Here is how.

Before you shop

  • Plan meals for the week. Choose five or six dinners, plus simple breakfast and lunch options. Build the shopping list from the plan, not the other way around.
  • Shop your kitchen first. Check what you already have, and design a meal or two around items that need using up.
  • Write a list and stick to it. Lists reduce impulse purchases, which are a main driver of grocery overspending.
  • Never shop hungry. Studies and plenty of personal experience suggest you buy more when you are hungry.

In the store

  • Compare unit prices. The price per 100 g, per litre or per unit often reveals that the bigger or "deal" package is not the cheapest.
  • Choose store brands. For many staples, quality is similar and the price is noticeably lower.
  • Buy seasonal produce. It is fresher and usually cheaper. Frozen fruit and vegetables are also good value and nutritious.
  • Look high and low on the shelves. The most expensive products tend to sit at eye level.
  • Use loyalty apps and digital coupons for items you were going to buy anyway.

Staples that stretch your budget

Some foods give a lot of nutrition and filling meals for little money. Rice, oats, dried or tinned beans and lentils, pasta, eggs, potatoes, cabbage, carrots, onions, bananas and frozen vegetables are all inexpensive. Build most dinners around two or three of these, then add smaller amounts of meat, fish, cheese or spices for variety.

Cut food waste

Food waste is wasted money. Store leftovers in clear containers at eye level, freeze bread, cooked rice and portions of soup, and label what you freeze. Use ripe fruit in smoothies or baking, and turn vegetable scraps into stock. Plan one "use it up" meal each week.

Cook in bulk

Batch cooking saves money and time. Make a large pot of chili, curry or pasta sauce, eat some now and freeze the rest. Having a meal ready also reduces the temptation of expensive takeaway on tired evenings.

A simple way to track progress

  1. Add up what you spent on groceries last month.
  2. Set a target 15% lower for the first month, then 25 to 30% over time.
  3. Check your spending weekly rather than monthly, so you can adjust early.
  4. Move the money you save to savings straight away.

If your grocery bill is $600 a month, a 30% cut saves $180 a month, or $2,160 a year. That is enough to build a starter emergency fund, as described in our guide to saving your first $1,000. Groceries belong in the "needs" part of your budget, so test your own numbers in the 50/30/20 budget calculator.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Buying in bulk items you will not use before they spoil.
  • Cutting so far that you resort to takeaway because you have nothing to cook.
  • Ignoring your health: cheap does not have to mean ultra-processed.
  • Changing everything at once. Start with meal planning and a list, then add one new habit every couple of weeks.
