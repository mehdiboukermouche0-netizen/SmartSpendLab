The Best Free Budgeting Methods for Gen Z and Millennials

You don’t need a paid app to budget well.

By SmartSpendLab Editorial TeamUpdated October 22, 20263 min read

You do not need to pay for software to manage your money. A budget is a method first and a tool second. If the method does not fit how you think, the fanciest app will end up unused. Here are five free methods, how each one works and who it suits best, so you can choose and start today.

1. The 50/30/20 rule

Split your take-home pay into 50% for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for savings and debt. It requires almost no tracking, which makes it ideal for beginners and people who dislike spreadsheets. The trade-off is that it is approximate. Try our 50/30/20 budget calculator to see your numbers, or read the full 50/30/20 guide.

2. Zero-based budgeting

Give every dollar a job so that income minus planned spending, saving and debt payments equals zero. It suits detail-oriented people, those with tight budgets and anyone who wants maximum control. It takes more effort each month. See how zero-based budgeting works.

3. The envelope method

Divide your spending money into categories such as groceries, fuel and entertainment, and use only what is in each envelope. Cash envelopes work well for people who overspend with cards. Digital versions use separate sub-accounts or pots in your bank app. When an envelope is empty, spending in that category stops until the next period.

4. Pay yourself first

Automate a fixed transfer to savings on payday, and spend what remains however you like. It is the simplest method and works well alongside any of the others. The trade-off is that it does not show you where the rest of the money goes.

5. A free spreadsheet

A basic sheet with columns for income, bills, spending categories and goals is powerful and fully customisable. It suits people who like control and are happy to enter data weekly. Many banks and free tools can also export transactions so you do not have to type everything.

How to choose

If you...Try
Want the simplest possible start50/30/20 or pay yourself first
Have a tight budget or irregular incomeZero-based budgeting
Overspend on cardsEnvelope method
Like detail and customisingSpreadsheet

What about budgeting apps?

Many banking apps now include free spending categories, alerts and savings pots. They are useful for visibility, but check what data they ask for, how the company makes money and whether they sell data. Use apps to support a method, not to replace one.

Tips to make it stick

  • Start with one method for one month before judging it.
  • Pick a fixed weekly time, ten minutes is enough, to check your numbers.
  • Automate savings and bill payments to remove decisions.
  • Allow a small fun budget so the plan feels sustainable.
  • Adjust, do not quit, when something changes. A budget is a living document.

The best budget is the one you will actually use. If you are not sure where to start, begin with the percentages, then move to a more detailed method if you need it.
