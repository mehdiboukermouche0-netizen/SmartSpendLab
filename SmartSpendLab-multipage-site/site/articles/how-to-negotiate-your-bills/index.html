A Step-by-Step Guide to Negotiating Your Utility and Internet Bills

Often a five-minute call is all it takes.

By SmartSpendLab Editorial TeamUpdated October 22, 20263 min read

Many providers will lower your bill if you simply ask. Telecoms, internet, insurance and some utilities compete hard for customers, and a short, polite call can save a meaningful amount every year. The most important step is preparation.

Which bills are worth negotiating?

  • Internet, mobile and TV packages
  • Car, home and contents insurance
  • Gym and club memberships
  • Software and streaming subscriptions
  • Some medical or service bills, where payment plans or reductions may be available

Bills that are set by regulation or contract, such as some council or property taxes, usually cannot be negotiated, though payment plans may be available.

Step-by-step: how to make the call

  1. Check what you pay now. Find your plan, price, contract end date and what is included.
  2. Research alternatives. Collect two or three current offers from competitors, ideally for a comparable service. Written quotes are best.
  3. Decide your goal. For example, "reduce my monthly price by $20" or "keep the same speed for a lower fee".
  4. Call and ask for the retention or loyalty team. These staff often have more authority to offer discounts.
  5. Be polite and clear. Say you have been a customer for X years, but you have received a better offer, and ask what they can do.
  6. Ask about extras. Promotions, bundles, loyalty discounts or removing services you do not use can all reduce the price.
  7. If the answer is no, ask for a supervisor or try again another day. Different agents have different options.
  8. Get the new price in writing. Confirm the monthly cost, the length of the promotional period and any new contract terms.

A short script to adapt

"Hello, I have been a customer for four years and I would like to review my plan. I have seen a similar package for $X a month. Is there anything you can do to bring my price closer to that? I would prefer to stay if we can find a better rate."

Know when to switch

If your provider will not match a competitor's offer and the savings are worth it, switching is a legitimate option. Check for early termination fees, installation costs and the price after any introductory period. Make sure service quality in your area is good before you leave.

Be wary of these traps

  • Discounts that disappear after six or twelve months. Set a calendar reminder before the end of the promotion.
  • Adding extra services in return for a small discount.
  • Longer contracts that lock you in for savings that are smaller than they sound.
  • Sharing sensitive details with callers who contacted you unprompted. Call the number on your bill instead.

Make it a yearly habit

Promotional rates often expire, and the price of your plan can quietly rise. Review your main bills once a year. If three bills each drop by $15 a month, that is $540 saved annually. Put the money to work, whether it goes toward your first $1,000 or the extra payment in the debt payoff planner. For other ways to cut costs, read our frugal living guide.
