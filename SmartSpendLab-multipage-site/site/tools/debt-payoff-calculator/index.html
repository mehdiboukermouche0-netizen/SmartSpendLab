Debt Payoff Planner

Enter up to five debts with their balances, interest rates and minimum payments, add any extra amount you can pay each month, and compare the avalanche and snowball methods.

Estimates for education only, not financial advice. Your numbers stay in your browser.

What this planner does

It simulates paying your debts month by month. Interest is added to each balance every month, minimum payments are made on every debt, and your extra payment goes to the target debt. When a debt is cleared, its minimum payment rolls into the next target, which is how both strategies speed up. The planner then shows your debt-free date, the total interest you would pay and how much you save compared with paying only the minimums.

Avalanche or snowball?

The avalanche method targets the debt with the highest interest rate first, which minimises total interest. The snowball method targets the smallest balance first, which clears whole debts sooner and can help motivation. Use the two tabs to compare them with your own numbers. Our guide to snowball versus avalanche explains which suits different personalities.

How to use it

  1. Enter the name, current balance, annual interest rate (APR) and minimum monthly payment for each debt. Leave unused rows at zero.
  2. Enter the extra amount you can add on top of your minimums each month.
  3. Switch between avalanche and snowball and note the debt-free date and total interest for each.
  4. Increase the extra payment in small steps to see what a few more dollars a month can do.

Worked example

Take a $3,000 credit card at 24%, a $6,000 car loan at 9% and a $1,500 personal loan at 15%, with minimum payments of $90, $150 and $45. Paying only the minimums takes about 50 months and costs about $3,550 in interest. Adding $200 a month cuts the time to about 25 months and the interest to roughly $1,400 with the avalanche method.

Things to keep in mind

The planner assumes fixed rates and no new borrowing. Credit cards often have variable rates, and some loans charge fees or penalties for early repayment, so check your terms. If a minimum payment is lower than the monthly interest, the balance will never fall, and the planner will tell you to increase your payments. This is an educational estimate, not advice. If you are struggling with debt, free non-profit credit counselling services can help.

Frequently asked questions

What does APR mean?

APR is the yearly interest rate on a debt. The planner divides it by 12 to calculate monthly interest on your remaining balance.

Why does it say to increase payments?

If your total payments are too small to cover the interest, your debt cannot be paid off within the simulated period. Raise the extra payment or the minimums.

Is my debt information stored or sent anywhere?

No. Everything stays in your browser and is not sent to a server.
