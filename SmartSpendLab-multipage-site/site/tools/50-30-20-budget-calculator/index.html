50/30/20 Budget Calculator

Enter your monthly take-home pay and see a simple, balanced split between needs, wants and savings. Switch presets if your living costs are higher than average.

Estimates for education only, not financial advice. Your numbers stay in your browser.

What this calculator does

The 50/30/20 rule divides your after-tax income into three groups. Needs, such as housing, food, utilities, transport, insurance and minimum debt payments, take about half. Wants, such as dining out, subscriptions, travel and hobbies, take about 30%. The remaining 20% goes to savings and extra debt repayment. This tool does the arithmetic instantly, so you can focus on the real question: does your actual spending match the plan?

How to use it

  1. Choose your currency and enter your monthly take-home pay, the amount that reaches your account after tax.
  2. Pick a split. The standard 50/30/20 works for many people. If rent is a large share of your income, try 60/20/20. If you want to save faster, try 40/30/30.
  3. Compare the results with your last month's bank statement. Add up what you spent on needs, wants and savings.
  4. Adjust the biggest gap first. If needs are over target, look for savings on housing, insurance or transport before cutting wants to zero.

Worked example

With a take-home pay of $3,500 a month, the standard split gives $1,750 for needs, $1,050 for wants and $700 for savings and debt repayment. Saving $700 a month for a year is $8,400, before any interest. Even if you can only manage half of that at first, the habit matters more than the exact amount.

When the rule needs adjusting

The percentages are a guide, not a law. In high-cost cities, needs can legitimately reach 60% or more, so wants shrink or savings grow more slowly. If you are paying off expensive debt, you might temporarily lower wants to push more into the third bucket. If you have a high income, 50% for needs may be generous, and you can raise the savings share. The point is to decide on purpose.

Limits of this tool

This calculator shows a target split, not your actual spending, and it does not know your taxes, benefits or irregular costs. Use it as a starting point and read our step-by-step 50/30/20 guide to turn the numbers into a monthly routine. Results are estimates for education only and not financial advice.

Frequently asked questions

Should I enter income before or after tax?

Use your take-home pay, the amount that actually reaches your account. If part of your salary goes to a workplace retirement plan before you are paid, count that as savings.

Where do debt payments go?

Minimum payments are needs because missing them has consequences. Extra payments above the minimum belong in the savings and debt share.

Does my income data get stored?

No. All calculations happen in your browser. Only your currency and language choices are saved on your device so the site remembers them next time.
