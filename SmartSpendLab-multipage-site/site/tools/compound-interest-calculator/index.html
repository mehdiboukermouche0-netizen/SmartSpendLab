Compound Interest Calculator

Enter a starting amount, a monthly contribution, an annual rate and a time period to see your estimated balance, your total contributions and the interest earned.

What this calculator does

Compound interest means you earn returns not only on the money you put in, but also on the returns you have already earned. This calculator estimates the future value of a starting deposit plus regular monthly contributions, with interest compounded monthly and each contribution added at the end of the month. It shows the final balance, how much you contributed yourself and how much came from interest, with a chart and a year-by-year table.

How to use it

  1. Choose your currency and enter the amount you already have, or zero if you are starting fresh.
  2. Set how much you can add each month. Consistency matters more than size.
  3. Enter an annual interest rate. For a savings account use its quoted rate. For long-term investing, try a few conservative values such as 4%, 6% and 8% rather than relying on a single optimistic one.
  4. Choose the number of years and watch how the green interest bars overtake the blue contribution bars in the later years.

The formula behind it

Each month the balance is multiplied by one plus the monthly rate, which is the annual rate divided by 12, and then the monthly contribution is added. Repeating that step for every month produces the final balance. You can reproduce the result in a spreadsheet with a simple running total.

Worked example

Starting with $1,000 and adding $100 a month at 6% a year gives about $8,300 after five years and about $18,200 after ten. Of the ten-year total, $13,000 is money you put in and about $5,200 is interest. Extend the same plan to 30 years and interest becomes the larger part of the balance.

What it does not include

The calculator ignores taxes, fees and inflation, and it assumes the same rate every year. Real returns on investments vary and can be negative in some years. Fees reduce what you keep, and inflation reduces what your future balance can buy. Savings account rates are usually variable. Treat the output as an illustration, not a forecast or a promise. Frequently asked questions

Is the interest compounded monthly?

Yes. The calculator applies one twelfth of the annual rate every month and adds your contribution at the end of each month.

What rate should I use?

For a savings account, use its current quoted rate. For investments, test several conservative rates and remember that returns are not guaranteed.

Does it account for inflation or taxes?

No. It shows nominal growth. To get a rough real-terms view, subtract your expected inflation rate from the interest rate you enter.
